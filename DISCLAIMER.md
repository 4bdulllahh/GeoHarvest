## You are responsible for how you use it

GeoHarvest reads information that businesses publish: their Google Maps listings and their
own websites. Reading public information is generally lawful, but the rules for collecting it
and contacting people differ between countries and services. **It is up to you** to make sure
your use is lawful where you and the businesses are. In particular:

- **Google's Terms of Service.** Google's terms limit automated access to its services. Use
  GeoHarvest at a reasonable pace (Settings > Search speed), and decide for yourself whether
  your use is acceptable to you.
- **Data protection.** Leads can include personal data, such as a sole trader's email address
  or an owner's name. Laws such as the **GDPR** (EU), the **UK GDPR**, the UAE's **Personal Data
  Protection Law**, Pakistan's data protection rules and US state privacy laws may apply to
  you as the person collecting and using it.
- **Marketing and spam laws.** If you contact the businesses you find, follow the rules for
  marketing messages, such as **CAN-SPAM** (US), **PECR** (UK), the **ePrivacy** rules (EU) and
  local laws: identify yourself, be honest, and let people opt out.
- **Websites' own terms.** Some websites forbid automated visits in their terms.

Do not use GeoHarvest to harass anyone, to send spam, to build lists for sale against these
rules, or for anything unlawful.

## Estimates can be wrong

Some details are estimates, not facts:

- **Company age** comes from when the website's domain was registered or first appeared
  online, not from when the company was founded.
- **The owner's name** is read from the website or the domain record and may be out of date,
  belong to someone else on the page, or be wrong.
- **AI scores** are an AI's opinion and can be mistaken.
- **Google's data** (opening hours, status, phone numbers) can be out of date.

Check anything important before you rely on it.
